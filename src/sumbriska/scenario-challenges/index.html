Simultaneous island flows case 1

Islands A, B and C begin with 12, 4 and 3 counters. Simultaneously, A sends 3 to B and B sends 2 to C. Each has enough starting counters for its outgoing transfer. What is B’s new total after both transfers settle?

Show the worked answer

A. 5

B receives 3 and sends 2, so its new total is 4+3−2=5. Both changes affect the same island. Counting only incoming or only outgoing counters omits part of the simultaneous update.

Island ledger case 1

An island begins with 8 counters. During a complete round it receives 5 counters from neighboring islands and sends out 3. No counters are created, lost or returned within that round. What ending balance should its ledger record?

Show the worked answer

B. 10

The ending balance is 8+5−3=10. Incoming transfers increase the island’s stock, and outgoing transfers decrease it. Their signs differ even though both kinds of movement cross a bridge connected to the island.

Two-at-a-time bridge case 1

An island begins with 3 counters. Its only allowed change is receiving exactly two counters per bridge crossing, with unlimited donor supply. No removals or smaller transfers are allowed. Can the island reach exactly 10 counters after some whole number of crossings?

Show the worked answer

C. No

Each crossing preserves whether the island total is odd or even. The required increase is 7, which is not divisible by two. Therefore the exact target cannot be reached using only two-counter additions.

Unequal target islands case 1

A begins with 12 counters and B with 2. Their targets are A=8 and B=6; equal totals are not required. Only transfers from A to B are allowed. How many counters must cross to satisfy both targets simultaneously?

Show the worked answer

B. 4

A must send 12−8=4, and B needs 6−2=4. The matching requirements show one transfer satisfies both. Equalizing the islands would answer a different question because these targets are deliberately unequal.

Counters spent at each crossing case 1

A traveler carries 9 counters across 2 bridges. Each crossing consumes 1 counters as a toll, permanently removing them from the carried load. No counters are gained elsewhere. How many counters remain with the traveler after all required crossings?

Show the worked answer

C. 7

2 tolls consume 2 counters, leaving 9−2=7. The toll repeats at every bridge rather than being charged once for the entire route. These counters are spent, so carried totals are not conserved.

Partial return ledger case 1

An island starts with 7 counters and sends 4 to a neighbor. Later the neighbor returns 1 of those counters, with no other traffic. How many counters does the original island hold after the return is complete?

Show the worked answer

A. 4

The island first falls to 3, then the return adds 1, giving 4. Only the returned amount comes back; the original outgoing transfer is not completely reversed. Tracking the two events separately avoids changing the wrong sign.

Missing incoming bridge case 1

An island starts with 6 counters, receives 3 through one bridge, receives an unknown amount through another, and sends 4 away. It finishes with 10. No other changes occur. How many counters arrived through the unknown incoming bridge?

Show the worked answer

B. 5

The known changes would leave 5, so the missing inflow must add 5 to reach 10. Substituting 5 gives 6+3+5−4=10. The unknown transfer is incoming, so it increases the balance.

Optional bridges used once case 1

An empty island may receive 2 counters from bridge A and 5 from bridge B. Each bridge can be used at most once, and either may be skipped. Transfers cannot be split. Can the island finish with exactly 7 counters?

Show the worked answer

C. Yes

The possible totals are 0, 2, 5, 7: use neither bridge, either one, or both. 7 appears in that list, so the target is reachable. Reordering the same chosen transfers does not change their sum.
