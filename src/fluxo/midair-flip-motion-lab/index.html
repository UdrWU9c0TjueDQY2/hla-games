USE IT IN REAL LIFE

The flip and the turn are two different moments.

Before the flip

The object begins at rest and gains upward speed. Negative velocity means upward movement in this diagram. Negative position means it is above its starting point.

At the flip

Acceleration switches from −1 to +1 unit/s². Position and velocity remain continuous: the object does not teleport or instantly reverse direction. While the velocity is still negative, the object keeps moving upward even though it is slowing down.

At the turn

Velocity reaches zero, then becomes positive. In this symmetric model, a flip at time f produces a turn at time 2f. A flip at 0.80 seconds therefore turns at 1.60 seconds. The highest position is −f² units, or −0.64 for that example.

The arithmetic behind the line

Before the flip: position = −t²/2 and velocity = −t.
After the flip: position = −f²/2 − f(t−f) + (t−f)²/2 and velocity = t−2f.

This is a deliberately small constant-acceleration model. Fluxo has game-specific movement, speed, collisions and hazards. The model explains a distinction; it does not tell you the correct tap time in the app.

Try three comparisons

  1. Set the flip to 0.40 seconds. Step to 0.80: velocity should be zero.
  2. Set it to 0.80. Step to 1.00: the pull is downward, but motion is still upward.
  3. Set it to 1.20. Compare the higher turning point at 2.40 seconds. More time accelerating upward means more momentum to reverse.
