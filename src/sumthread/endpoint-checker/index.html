Can the path finish there?

Pick two squares. Can you travel from one to the other and visit every square exactly once? Check what the alternating colors rule out, then see what an actual route proves.

Why shade the squares?

A horizontal or vertical step always changes color. On nine squares, the start and finish must both use the color that appears five times. On a board with equal numbers of both colors, the ends must have opposite colors.

Passing that check is only a first test. Try the ladder with B1 and B2 as the ends. Both colors are represented, yet those two middle squares separate the left side from the right. A single path between the middle ends cannot cover both sides without revisiting an end.
