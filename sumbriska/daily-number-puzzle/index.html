What is this puzzle, in one breath?

It is the core rule of Sumbriska, the iPhone number puzzle we make: islands have target sums, bridges carry numbers, and flipping a bridge moves its number from one island to the other. There is no arithmetic beyond single-digit addition. The skill is seeing which island is short, which is over, and which bridge fixes both at once.

  • Daily: one board per calendar day, same for everyone, replayable.
  • Short: five islands and eight bridges. Par is the fewest flips from the starting arrows to any valid solution.
  • Honest: the solution is one tap away and nothing is tracked.

Can two solutions take different numbers of flips?

Yes. On the April 10 example, two flips solve the board. A second solution takes five. Both end with exactly the same total on every island.

See the two moves and the extra loop

Turn the 9 from island 1 toward island 2. Turn the 8 from island 5 toward island 4. The five totals now read 5, 13, 13, 12 and 4. No single move can fix all four islands that start off target.

For a second solution, send a 4 from island 2 to 3, a 4 from 3 to 4, and a 4 from 4 back to 2. Each of those islands gives away exactly what it receives. That loop explains why two different arrow patterns can produce the same totals.

How a board goes, in three steps

Find the island that is furthest off

Compare each island's sum with its target. The biggest gap usually names the bridge to flip first.

Flip the bridge that helps both ends

A flip subtracts from one island and adds to the other. The best flips fix an island that is over and one that is under in a single tap.

Check the remainder, then the tide

The grand total always stays the same. If one island has too much, at least one other island has too little. Trace the bridges between them. In the app, Two Tides boards make you check the same layout against a second schedule.

Questions people ask

How does the daily number puzzle work?

Each island shows a target. Each bridge carries a number and points at one island; that island counts the number in its sum. Tap a bridge to flip which island it points at. When every island's sum equals its target, the board is solved. A new board appears every day, built from the date, so everyone gets the same puzzle.

Is the puzzle on this page the same as the app?

It uses the same core rule, flip bridges to match sums, with five islands and eight bridges. The iPhone app adds themed regions, Two Tides boards that must satisfy two schedules at once, timed Harbor Run, the endless Deep, a workshop for custom boards, and a harbor you build as you solve.

Is Sumbriska free?

Yes to download and play. Regions 1 and 2, the Daily board, The Deep and Harbor Run are included. An optional Voyage Pass subscription ($4.99 a month or $29.99 a year, no free trial) adds regions 3 to 6, side boards, Gale Run, the 3-4 ring workshop and cosmetics.

Is it suitable for kids?

The listing describes child profiles with no purchases and no links, an adult area behind PIN or Face ID, no ads, no accounts and no data collection, and it works offline. The board on this page collects nothing either.

What is the minimum number of flips?

Each of the eight bridges can point two ways. The game checks all 256 arrangements, keeps those that meet all five targets, and finds the smallest number of arrows that must change from the start. That minimum is par. You can solve the puzzle using more flips.
