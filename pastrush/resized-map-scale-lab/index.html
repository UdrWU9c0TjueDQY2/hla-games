USE IT IN REAL LIFE

The geography stayed still. The paper changed.

A ratio scale compares the same units: at 1:50,000, one centimetre on the original map represents 50,000 centimetres, or 500 metres. USGS explains the ratio-scale convention.

At 150% print size, the same feature becomes 1.5 times as long on paper. Divide the original denominator by 1.5. For 3 cm on that enlarged copy, the example distance is 1 km, not 1.5 km.

A graphic scale bar enlarged along with the map can still be measured against the copy. A printed ratio label does not update itself. “Fit to page” may resize by an unknown amount: do not guess the percentage.

This calculation assumes uniform resizing and an appropriate local scale. A stretched image, projection distortion or an inaccurate historical map can break those assumptions. A straight-line distance also says nothing about actual routes or travel time.

What if I do not know the print percentage?

Compare a known feature length on the original and the copy, using the same units: copy length divided by original length, times 100. Without a trustworthy reference, this tool cannot recover the scale.
